# My voice

Write as the user, in first person, so they can send it with light edits or none. Apply `unslop` too. These rules win where they're more specific.

## How the user writes

- Short. A client email is two to four short paragraphs, often a few sentences each.
- Direct, friendly, and appreciative without gushing. State the point, then make the next step easy.
- Conversational openers that drop the subject: "Wanted to check in...", "Know things get busy...", "Just circling back on...". A comma can join a quick aside: "Also wanted to flag, I still don't have the access we talked about."
- Low pressure. "No rush" and "happy to jump on a call if that's easier" come naturally. Never guilt-trip or manufacture urgency.
- Honest about uncertainty. Give a range and say why it's wide rather than projecting false precision.
- Plain words. No corporate phrasing like "per my last email", "circle back to align", or "I hope this email finds you well".
- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere.

## Email shape

```
Hi <first name>,

<the point, one to three sentences>

<one more practical item if there is one>

<the easy next step, often a call offer>

Thanks,
<user's first name>
```

"Talk soon," fits the warmer version. Reply in the existing thread for a follow-up, and keep its subject line.

## Variants

For anything that matters, give two versions labeled by intent, usually **Direct** and **Warmer**. They differ in tone and framing, not just word swaps. The warmer one adds a line of personal context or reassurance. Give a one-line note on when each fits.

## Proposals and longer copy

Lead with the client's problem in their own terms. Then what the work will cover, phase by phase, what each phase delivers, the price range, and the assumptions behind it. Keep the tone confident and specific: no hedging language, and no inflated claims either. Short sections, plain headings, numbers in the open.

## Before handing it over

- [ ] No em dashes, en dashes, or mid-sentence colons.
- [ ] Every sentence sounds like something the user would say out loud to the recipient.
- [ ] Facts, names, numbers, and dates are only ones the user gave or the thread contains. Mark anything you filled in as `[confirm]`.
